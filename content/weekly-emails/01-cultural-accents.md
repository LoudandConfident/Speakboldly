## 1 — Cultural عقدة خواجة: Why do we judge our own accents?

In Egypt, we sometimes make it difficult for each other to simply be ourselves.

I don’t know when mixing up “B” and “P,” or pronouncing “th” differently, became something to mock. Yet some of the same people readily respect an Indian speaking English or warmly encourage an American learning Arabic.

Why don’t we offer each other that same encouragement?

Learning another language—even when the job market makes it feel compulsory—is challenging. It also opens your mind, introduces you to different cultures, and gives you new ways to express yourself. That effort deserves respect.

There’s nothing shameful about your Arabic showing up in your English accent. You can work on pronunciation so people understand you clearly while still sounding like yourself.

Before you laugh at someone’s pronunciation, remember: they’re making the effort to communicate in another language. A little encouragement could help them keep going.

**Your accent carries your story. It shouldn’t silence your voice.**
